# Analysis summary

All component tests below use two-sided, unmodified *p* values. Confidence intervals are 95% intervals.

## Study 1

### Sample description

Study 1 contains 230 participants. The pre-adolescent cohort comprised 104 participants recruited through Grade 4 classes; its mean age was 9.93 years (*SD* = 0.25, variance = 0.063, range = 9–10), and 51.0% were female. The middle-adolescent cohort comprised 126 participants recruited through Grade 8 classes; among the 117 participants with observed age, mean age was 14.03 years (*SD* = 0.50, variance = 0.249, range = 13–16), and 50.8% of the full cohort were female. The observed-age difference was 4.09 years, 95% CI [3.99, 4.20], *F*(1, 219) = 5697.76, *p* < .001. Gender composition was similar across cohorts, χ²(1) < 0.01, *p* = .980.

### Story-condition check

The purpose of this analysis is to determine whether changes differ between the two Study 1 story conditions before pooling them. Story-condition differences were not detected for ln(*k*), immediate allocation, short-term allocation, long-term allocation, or subjective feeling of time passage; all *p* values were at least .064.

### Baseline developmental cohort difference

The purpose of this analysis is to describe whether the two developmental cohorts differed in delay discounting before the session. Baseline ln(*k*) was higher in the middle-adolescent cohort (*M* = -5.76) than in the pre-adolescent cohort (*M* = -6.85), mean difference = 1.09, 95% CI [0.56, 1.62], Welch *t*(219.82) = 4.05, *p* < .001, Hedges *g* = 0.53.

### Developmental cohort comparison of pre-post change

The purpose of the multivariate resource-allocation analysis is to test the cohort difference in the overall immediate–short-term allocation pattern while avoiding redundancy from the three percentages summing to 100. The joint cohort difference was significant, Pillai's trace = .053, *F*(2, 227) = 6.35, *p* = .002.

The middle-adolescent cohort showed a 5.78-point larger increase in immediate allocation than the pre-adolescent cohort, 95% CI [1.46, 10.11], Welch *t*(226.37) = 2.64, *p* = .009. The middle-adolescent cohort also showed a 7.57-point larger decline in short-term allocation, 95% CI [-12.43, -2.72], Welch *t*(199.12) = -3.08, *p* = .002. Cohort differences in ln(*k*), long-term allocation, and subjective-feeling-of-time-passage change did not reach .05.

Within the pre-adolescent cohort, ln(*k*) increased by 0.37, *p* = .028; immediate allocation increased by 3.32 points, *p* = .034; short-term allocation increased by 3.64 points, *p* = .068; long-term allocation decreased by 6.96 points, *p* = .003; and subjective feeling of time passage decreased by 5.04 points, *p* = .005.

Within the middle-adolescent cohort, ln(*k*) increased by 0.75, *p* < .001; immediate allocation increased by 9.10 points, *p* < .001; short-term allocation decreased by 3.94 points, *p* = .009; long-term allocation decreased by 5.17 points, *p* = .002; and subjective feeling of time passage decreased by 3.58 points, *p* = .016.

### Pooled pre-post description

The purpose of this analysis is to document the full Study 1 pre-post pattern represented alongside Study 2 in Figures 2–4. Across 230 participants, ln(*k*) increased by 0.58, 95% CI [0.34, 0.82], *t*(229) = 4.77, *p* < .001; immediate allocation increased by 6.49 points, 95% CI [4.29, 8.69], *t*(229) = 5.81, *p* < .001; short-term allocation changed by -0.51 points, 95% CI [-2.93, 1.90], *t*(229) = -0.42, *p* = .677; long-term allocation decreased by 5.98 points, 95% CI [-8.64, -3.31], *t*(229) = -4.42, *p* < .001; and subjective feeling of time passage decreased by 4.24 points, 95% CI [-6.46, -2.01], *t*(229) = -3.75, *p* < .001.

## Study 2

### Story-condition check

The purpose of this analysis is to determine whether pre-post changes differ among the three Study 2 stories. No story-condition difference was detected for the five core outcomes or implementation intention; all *p* values were at least .579.

### Core pre-post outcomes

The purpose of these analyses is to describe the changes observed after the educational session. Across 184 participants, ln(*k*) increased by 0.410, 95% CI [0.082, 0.737], *t*(183) = 2.47, *p* = .015. Immediate allocation increased by 7.38 points, 95% CI [4.27, 10.50], *t*(183) = 4.67, *p* < .001. Short-term allocation decreased by 3.08 points, 95% CI [-5.91, -0.26], *t*(183) = -2.16, *p* = .032. Long-term allocation decreased by 4.30 points, 95% CI [-8.06, -0.54], *t*(183) = -2.26, *p* = .025. Subjective feeling of time passage decreased by 3.16 points, 95% CI [-5.56, -0.76], *t*(183) = -2.59, *p* = .010.

The joint immediate–short-term allocation change was significant, *F*(2, 182) = 11.61, *p* < .001. This multivariate analysis tests whether the allocation profile changed while representing the compositional outcome with two nonredundant components.

### Stated purposes and SS/LL choices

The purpose-distribution analysis describes how participants intended to use the selected money. Saving for the future was the most frequent purpose at pretest (63.0% of all item responses) and posttest (61.1%). Among SS responses, saving for the future accounted for 61.8% at pretest and 57.5% at posttest; immediate consumption accounted for 15.3% and 17.7%, respectively.

The participant-clustered logistic model tests whether SS choice is associated with assessment time point and stated purpose while accounting for item differences and repeated responses from each participant. The omnibus assessment-time-point effect was χ²(1) = 2.48, *p* = .115; the purpose effect was χ²(4) = 8.47, *p* = .076; and the assessment time point × purpose interaction was χ²(4) = 2.60, *p* = .627. Item differences were detected, χ²(6) = 180.71, *p* < .001.

### Implementation intention

The paired analysis tests whether average stated implementation intention changed across assessment time points. Mean intention increased by 0.078 points, 95% CI [0.034, 0.121], *t*(183) = 3.54, *p* < .001.

The participant-clustered linear model tests implementation intention as a function of assessment time point, SS/LL choice, stated purpose, and item. The omnibus assessment-time-point effect was χ²(1) = 16.09, *p* < .001; the choice effect was χ²(1) = 3.70, *p* = .054; the purpose effect was χ²(4) = 9.56, *p* = .049; and the assessment time point × choice interaction was χ²(1) = 2.32, *p* = .128.

## Design boundary

These are pre-post comparisons without a neutral or no-session control group. They describe changes observed across the educational sessions.
